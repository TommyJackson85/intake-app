## 1. Pilot hypothesis

**Operational problem being tested**

A Florida real-estate firm may receive a vague property-tax-related enquiry and need to determine whether it is an assessment/VAB, buyer tax-estimate, or delinquent-tax/tax-deed matter; collect the appropriate notices/documents; record uncertain dates; and route the matter to the correct reviewer.

**Target buyer / user**

- **Buyer:** Florida real-estate law firm (solo to mid-size) whose public practice includes property-tax/VAB, transaction/investor closing, title, tax-deed-adjacent litigation, and/or probate/heirship work involving notices.
- **Primary users:** intake coordinator, paralegal/closing coordinator, associate triaging facts, practice lead who sets routing standards.

**Smallest testable value proposition**

Separating vague “property-tax” intakes into three tracks, capturing facts and date-verification states, and suggesting staff-controlled document requests reduces misrouting and document chasing—without the software giving advice or calculating outcomes.

**Evidence that would validate the problem**

- Prospects independently describe recent intake confusion (assessment vs closing vs tax-deed).
- They volunteer missing notices/documents before attorney review.
- They describe uncertain or unverified notice dates and unclear ownership of next review.
- They react positively to track separation and date-verification states in a fictional demo.
- At least some agree to a follow-up workflow review or controlled fictional/demo test.

**Evidence that would disconfirm the problem**

- Most firms say these enquiries are rare, already cleanly routed, or always referred out with no internal pain.
- Document/date chasing is not a recurring issue they care about.
- They only want calculators, e-filing, surplus recovery, or legal-advice automation.
- They reject fictional/demo feedback and will not look at sample matters.
- After discovery calls, no recurring intake/document/date/routing pain is identified.

## 3. Qualification scorecard

Score each prospect **0–10** using **observable public signals only** (firm website, public bios, public LinkedIn company/profile text). Do not invent volume or pain from marketing copy alone.

| Points | Observable public signal |
|--------|--------------------------|
| +2 | Publicly mentions VAB, ad valorem, assessment appeals, exemptions, property taxes, or property-appraiser disputes |
| +2 | Publicly mentions tax deeds, tax certificates, title litigation, foreclosure, probate/heirship, or surplus proceeds |
| +2 | Serves investors, developers, commercial owners, landlords, condo associations, or complex transaction clients |
| +2 | Has multiple staff/attorneys, an intake team, a closing team, or a clear multi-person workflow |
| +1 | Has a relevant Florida geographic focus (state, metro, or county) |
| +1 | Shows an active online presence or an appropriate professional contact route |

**Outreach tiers**

| Tier | Score | Action |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Tier A** | 8–10 | Prioritize for tailored discovery outreach |
| **Tier B** | 5–7 | Secondary list; personalize carefully |
| **Tier C** | 0–4 | Do not prioritize; use only as comparison or skip |

**Warning:** Practice-area marketing is **not** proof of matter volume or operational pain. A firm that lists “property tax” or “tax deeds” may still refer all such work out, handle it rarely, or have no intake confusion. Treat public signals as **eligibility filters**, then test pain only on calls.

## 5. Personalisation framework

Messages ask for **feedback**, not a sale. Open with a public observation, one clear question, and an easy out.

### Safe opening patterns — property-tax / VAB practices

1. “Your site mentions VAB / assessment work—I’m validating how Florida firms intake TRIM and assessment enquiries before attorney review. Would a short feedback call be useful?”
2. “Noticing your public focus on exemptions / property-appraiser disputes. Curious how staff separate those from closing or tax-deed calls when the client just says ‘property tax.’”
3. “I’m researching operational intake for Florida assessment matters (fictional demo only). Open to 20 minutes on what documents and dates are usually missing?”
4. “Your practice pages list ad valorem / VAB. I’m not selling—just checking whether clearer issue tracks would reduce misrouting on vague tax enquiries.”
5. “If assessment intakes often arrive without notices or petition status, I’d value your take on a fictional Assessment/VAB workflow demo.”

### Safe opening patterns — investor / transaction practices

1. “Your firm publicly works with investor / LLC buyers. Do those files ever rely on the seller’s current tax bill as the future estimate—and is that flagged at intake?”
2. “I’m validating a Florida closing-team workflow demo (fictional data) around buyer tax-estimate risk. Would feedback from your closing coordinator help?”
3. “Saw commercial / developer / condo association language on your site. Curious whether post-purchase tax-estimate questions create intake confusion.”
4. “Seeking Florida transaction-firm feedback on separating buyer tax-estimate review from VAB and tax-deed matters—learning, not pitching.”
5. “If investor closings generate follow-up tax questions, I’d appreciate 20 minutes on how you request docs and verify dates today.”

### Safe opening patterns — tax-deed / title / probate-adjacent practices

1. “Your public pages mention tax deeds / title litigation / heirship. I’m researching how former-owner or heir notices are triaged at intake.”
2. “Curious whether Clerk surplus-related notices arrive with incomplete ownership documents—and how staff record uncertain dates.”
3. “Validating a fictional Florida tax-deed/surplus intake track for learning. Would a short call with your paralegal or litigator be appropriate?”
4. “Not asking for a purchase—want to know if separating tax-deed work from VAB and ordinary closings would help your desk.”
5. “If probate/heirship matters sometimes include tax-deed or surplus notices, your feedback on document presets and date-verification states would help.”

### Poor personalisation lines to avoid

1. “I know you have this problem.”
2. “This will ensure compliance.”
3. “This will prevent missed deadlines.”
4. “This automates VAB/tax-deed cases.”
5. “This gives legal advice” / “This recovers surplus funds” / “This integrates with your existing software” (unless a real integration exists—and it does not in this demo).

### Checklist — only claim what the firm publicly advertises

- [ ] I opened the firm’s own website (or clearly public profile) and noted the exact practice phrases used.
- [ ] My message only references practice areas or client types those pages advertise.
- [ ] I did **not** claim they handle every issue track or have high volume.
- [ ] I did **not** imply they are a customer or that competitors already use the product.
- [ ] I ask for feedback / learning, not a demo close or purchase.
- [ ] I avoid compliance, deadline-prevention, automation, legal-advice, surplus-recovery, and fake-integration claims.
- [ ] Contact name and role were verified from a public source (or left as firm general contact).

## 8. Pilot decision rule

- **Continue testing** if at least **3 of 10** prospects independently identify a recurring intake / document / date / routing pain.
- **Prioritise a pilot** if at least **2** firms agree to a follow-up workflow review or controlled test.
- **Do not build firm-configurable routing** until at least **3** firms ask for different default owners, document packs, terminology, or internal procedures.
- **If most firms refer property-tax / tax-deed matters out**, retain the feature as a triage differentiator and refocus engineering on condo diligence, closing workflow, estoppels, SIRS/milestone review, AML/FinCEN, and core document handoffs.
